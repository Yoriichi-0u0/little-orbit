# Little Orbit: research and challenge mapping

Research checked on 4 October 2026. All science references below are NASA or NASA JPL primary sources. These are explanatory sources, not a live telemetry feed.

## Challenge

[Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/build-a-junior-astronaut-mission-trainer/) asks for an interactive game or app in which students run a lunar or Martian outpost while balancing life support, radiation shielding, power, and food production. The publicly visible Details tab currently provides the challenge summary, difficulty, and subjects; it does not provide additional technical requirements or a dataset. The implementation interprets the summary as the acceptance criteria.

## Boundaries of the model

- Resource values are normalized game units, not liters, kilograms, kilowatt-hours, medical risk, or engineering requirements. Credits and cargo points are game constraints.
- Hardware rates, crew demands, exact event dates, rewards, and resource capacities are original balance settings. NASA sources support the relationships, not the numeric calibration.
- Growing crops takes much longer in reality. The game's short maturation period compresses time to make the dependency visible within a 14-turn lesson.
- Mars solar reduction is a teaching parameter, not a calculation of actual irradiance or weather at a geographic landing site. Lunar shadow timing is an authored scenario, not an ephemeris.
- Hazards are scripted, reproducible learning scenarios. They are not space-weather forecasts.
- Shelter effectiveness and health loss are game mechanics. They must not be interpreted as radiation dose predictions or astronaut health guidance.
- The isometric outpost art is an original AI-generated illustration, not NASA photography, measured terrain, or a literal engineering diagram.
- Little Orbit is an independent Space Apps project. It is not an official NASA product and is not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA.

## Learning design

Students predict the next turn, make an allocation or construction decision, observe the change, and inspect the explanation. The mission journal keeps resource history and decision consequences. Flight school supplies linked science explanations, short retrieval questions, and classroom discussion prompts. A downloadable debrief supports comparing two runs with different choices.

No accounts, public ranking, or server-side student information are needed. Progress and completed mission records are saved locally on the current browser/device. Clearing browser storage removes them.
